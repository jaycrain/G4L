# THE SPINE — read this before both parts

**This is the single most important thing in the brief.** It is the organising idea behind both pages, and until
this week we did not have it. It is Greg's, from his 2026-08-08 email, and it is his life's work restated for the
product — his textbook is *Concepts of Fitness and Wellness*:

> *"The linkage that is in one of my 'Cycle documents' is that **W builds mindfulness, B builds fitness, and C
> builds wellness. The three outcomes are all 'products' (i.e. nouns - something you have)**. The first pass
> through Cycle 1 would help members track behaviors (i.e. the process) that build the skills needed to achieve
> the product. It is like shooting free throws as practice to refine the skill. Our first pass through all three
> of these Rs will build an introduction to all three. **Mindfulness and Fitness both directly contribute to
> Wellness so they build hierarchically too.** … The novelty of building mindfulness and wellness are
> differentiators as **other apps only focus on diet and activity.**"*

## What this gives us

| Phase | Builds | Which is a… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rewire** | mindfulness | product — a noun, something you *have* |
| **Rebuild** | fitness | product |
| **Reclaim** | wellness | product — **and the one the other two feed** |

**It is hierarchical, not a list.** Mindfulness and fitness both contribute to wellness. That is why the phases
are in that order, and it is the first explanation we have ever had for the sequence that isn't just "this is the
order we built them in."

**Process vs product is the load-bearing distinction.** Cycle 1 does not hand a member wellness. It has them
track *behaviours* — the process — which build the *skills*, which over time produce the *product*. Greg's
free-throw analogy is the clearest version: you practise the shot, you don't practise winning.

This is already in the product in exactly one place, C3's science panel, in Greg's own construction:
**"Healthy days are the process; wellness is the product."** That sentence is the model for the whole idea.

## Why it matters for these two pages specifically

- **The Program / Cycle 1 page** is where a member should understand what they are *building* across the cycle,
  and in what order, and why that order. Today it lists activities. This gives it a spine.
- **The Playbook** is where a member sees what they *have*. Jay's framing: *"The Playbook needs to demonstrate
  the 'outcomes' of the 4 Rs in useful and practical way."* These three products are those outcomes.

## Two things to hold while you write it

**It is not in the product yet.** No member-facing surface names mindfulness / fitness / wellness as a trio. You
are writing toward it, which Jay has greenlit for these two pages — but nothing should read as though a member
has already been told this.

**Wellness is a noun. Always.** Never a verb, never a score, never "your wellness level", never "wellness" as a
vague good feeling. It drives Greg genuinely mad and he is right: **wellness is the outcome of healthy
lifestyles**, and healthy lifestyles are built from fundamentals — the simple things people have got out of the
habit of doing. Midlife identity loss skews you away from those things; a lifetime of small bad decisions adds
up. G4L reframes that. That is also the symmetry with the **Lifestyle Pilot** in Cycle 1: a few things going
right, building momentum.

**And the positioning that falls out of it, in Jay's words:** no GLP, no bio-hacking, no miracle treatments. Not
that everything about them is wrong — there is just a simpler, proven way. *"Maybe harder, maybe not as
glamourous. But effective."* If you want a spine for how G4L differs from everything else in this market, that is
it, and Greg's line supports it: other apps only do diet and activity.

# PART 1 — The Program page

## What it is becoming

Jay: *"The Program is a glorified Syllabus, necessary but not the most valuable. Just outlines the Program until
it doesn't."*

That last clause is the whole brief. A syllabus is accurate while a member walks Cycle 1 **in order**. The moment
they are in the Loop — cycling back, running things out of sequence — a syllabus cannot describe what they are
doing. So the page stops being "the whole curriculum" and becomes **the view of the cycle they are in now.**

**"Program" stays the word in the top nav. Permanently.** Jay was explicit: nav that renames itself as cycles
increment is churn. The word *Cycle 1* belongs on the page, not in the navigation.

Also new, and relevant: **Program is now the first crumb of the dashboard breadcrumb** —
`Program › Reclaim › 3 of 4 sessions › Checkpoint ready`. That is the first time the word does work *inside* the
product rather than naming a page you visit. Jay's reasoning: *"The Program is the biggest idea, and while it is
the marketing term it still needs to pay off and connect with the app."* Your copy should feel like the
destination that breadcrumb promises.

## The one fact we have never told a member

Greg (2026-08-07): *"We can fine-tune and explain on the front end that it is about a **6 week experience for
Cycle 1**."*

**We state this nowhere.** A member has no idea how long any of this takes. That is a real gap and probably the
single most useful thing this page could say.

Two cautions. Nothing in the engine paces a member yet — Greg walked the whole cycle in seven days — so the copy
should frame six weeks as **the shape it is designed for**, not a promise the product enforces. And Greg's own
qualifier: Level 3 activities are a full week each; Level 2 has *"latitude depending on the member"*.

## What Cycle 2 is (so the copy doesn't overclaim)

Greg: *"ONCE A MEMBER IS INTO CYCLE 2, THEY WILL HAVE THE FOUNDATIONAL SKILLS AND WILL BE WORKING THROUGH **OTHER
ACTIVITIES** OR ENGAGING WITH THE COMPANION."*

Cycle 2 is **not** the same twelve assets again. Don't write anything implying a member repeats this. Cycle 1
builds the foundational skills; what follows is different work.

## The ask

- A short framing for the page: what a cycle is, what this one holds, roughly how long it is designed to take.
- Section copy for the four phases **that does not duplicate** `lib/content/summaries.ts` — those phase summaries
  are canon and already appear here. Yours should frame the *arc*, not re-describe each phase.
- A line for the member who has finished Cycle 1 and is looking at this page. We have nothing for that today.

# PART 2 — The Playbook

## Read this before writing a word

The Playbook was a scrapbook and we were about to make it a better-organised scrapbook. Jay stopped that:

> *"A Playbook isn't a historical archive. It's an active tool. Football teams don't create a Playbook after the
> season and put it on a shelf. They write it before the season and plan games with it, and modify it when things
> don't work or new things get figured out. They are super sophisticated in the NFL now and live on iPads."*

**The inversion: the Playbook is not the output of the work. It is the instrument the work is done with.**

| The old model (wrong) | What it actually is |
| --- | --- |
| fills up as you finish Sessions | **exists from the start**, and gets richer |
| holds what you finished | holds **what you can run**, and **what you're running now** |
| you read it | you **plan from it**, before the week |
| entries are records | plays are **revised when they don't work** |
| a destination you visit | a tool you **use in the moment** |

Jay's standing view: it should be **the most valuable asset G4L produces on behalf of a member.**

## Forbidden framings

**Never "journal", never "scrapbook", never "archive", never "your story so far" as the organising idea.** Those
describe what we are moving away from. (There is a Journal *inside* the Playbook — see below — but the Playbook
is not one.)

Also avoid anything backward-leaning as the dominant tense: "a record of", "look back on", "everything you've
done". The dominant tense is **forward and present**: what you can run, what you're running, what to call next.

## What it holds — four things, not one

1. **The plays** — what you can run. Their true lines, their image, their False Start Protocol, their Lifestyle
   Pilot. Each carries a *situation* ("When the old story starts talking") and a **"Run it again with your
   Companion →"** — this already works, including a count of how many times they've come back to it.
2. **What you're running now** — the live practice week. **This is new and it is a decision from today:** the
   practice weeks are moving here (see below).
3. **The reads** — the scouting report. What tells you *which* play to call. Mostly unbuilt.
4. **The record** — closed weeks, revised plays and what changed. Present, but **not the front door.**

## A decision from this morning that changes the Playbook's job

Jay: *"Much like Field Guide, Momentum may be a relic… I'd created it so there was SOME daily interaction for a
member to have. I believe we've replaced that notion with much more sophisticated and valuable daily
interactions."*

So **the practice weeks move into the Playbook**, and Momentum becomes a long view for later cycles rather than
the daily surface. This is what makes the Playbook the page a member opens most: it is where the week they are
actually running lives.

**Do not write about Momentum going away.** It is not being removed and nothing has changed for a member yet.

## The Journal — do not under-play it

Jay has been explicit about this more than once. The Journal is a **first-class, highly productive reflective
tool** for members who naturally write — thoughts and feelings, time-stamped to the Session just finished. It is
personally load-bearing for him (*"sets me free… understand myself"*). It has two jobs, both real: feedstock for
what the Companion keeps, **and its own reward.** Present, never demoted.

## The ask — voice, not tab labels

**Please don't write per-tab UI strings yet.** The Playbook's structure has moved three times in 24 hours and
anything keyed to a specific tab will be thrown away. What I need is the layer that survives:

- **What the Playbook IS**, in a few sentences a member reads once and understands. The hardest and most valuable
  piece. The NFL metaphor is the source of the idea — it is not necessarily the language a 50-year-old woman
  reclaiming "the Swimmer" wants. Find what carries the same meaning for her.
- **The empty state.** A member finishing Reconnect should already have a Playbook — their identity, their doors,
  their Reclaim List are the opening plays. Today it fills only as keepers are lifted, so **the most valuable
  thing we produce is emptiest exactly when a member is newest.** What does it say then?
- **The invitation to plan.** "Plan games with it" is the missing verb. Before a hard week, travel, the holidays —
  what does the Playbook say to prompt that? Right now, nothing.
- **How a revised play reads.** When something didn't work and they changed it, the *change* is more useful than
  the play alone. We have no language for that at all.

# Constraints for both pages

**Locked vocabulary — use these, invent nothing:** the 4Rs (Reconnect · Rewire · Rebuild · Reclaim), the Fade,
the Door, the Reclaim List, the ID Score, the Grinta Index, the Loop, the Playbook, the Community, the Beat, the
close, Grinta, a Comeback.

- **The 4Rs are ONE capital.** Reconnect, not ReConnect. Rebuild, not ReBuild. (Greg's house style leaks it in
  from every science doc — it is wrong for us.)
- **The Community**, never "Connect". The code path says `connect`; a member must never see that word as a noun.
- **Wellness is a noun and an outcome** — see THE SPINE above, which is the fuller version. Never a verb, never a
  score, never "your wellness". Same for **mindfulness** and **fitness**: all three are things you *have*.
- **A member's reclaimed identity is natural case** — "the Athlete", never all-caps.

**Voice — from `voice-rules.md` in the bundle, plus three Jay has been actively cutting:**

- **Never "it's not X, it's Y."** Declare what a thing IS. The only negation that stays is Fade-normalising
  ("a hundred reasonable decisions, not a failing").
- **Never "honest / honestly"** as a softener — it reads as an AI tell.
- **No "That's ___ done"** hand-off filler.
- Plain, measured, no hype. Normalising, never motivational-pep, never corrective. Sound like a person, not a brand.

**Never a verdict.** Nothing on either page grades a member, ranks them, or implies they are behind. This is the
hardest one and it is absolute: a surface that scolds is one people stop opening, and then we lose the member
*and* the data.
