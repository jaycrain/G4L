## 1. The primer — new "before we start" beat

**Where:** a short beat at onboarding open — alongside the AI-disclosure beat (Task 2), before the first conversation message.

**SHIP:**

> **Before we start**
>
> This is the foundation for everything that follows, so give it room. Set aside about **15–20 minutes**, get comfortable, and treat it like a conversation — not a form. There are no wrong answers, nothing is timed, and you can stop and pick up where you left off any time.
>
> Here's how it goes: first we talk for a few minutes about who you are and what's pulled you off course. Then a short questionnaire. Then your first **ID Score** — a real number for how far you've drifted from yourself, and the starting line you'll watch move.

## 4. The identity-naming sequence — pace it

The agent-led moment that narrows to a one-word identity, then asks what the member wants back. Two abrupt jumps to fix.

**Behavior (narrow gradually, reflect-then-ask):**
- Don't jump from the abstract opening question straight to "give me one word." Add a beat: reflect what they've said, offer a couple of candidate words **drawn from their own language**, then invite them to choose or coin one. Frame the word as a **handle — changeable**, not a verdict.
- After they lock the word, **add a connecting line** that gives it meaning and bridges to the next ask.

**SHIP (connecting line, with the member's data):** `[dynamic]`

> Locked. {Identity} is the version of you that feels most like yourself. Now let's talk about what you want back from {them}. Three to start — more if they keep coming.

## 6. The Door prompt — slow it down

**NOW:** "One more thing before the work starts — and it might be the most important: how did the gap open? Something usually does it, and rarely all at once. Tell me what happened — when you first felt the drift, and what it quietly cost you."

**SHIP:**

> One more thing before we start the work — and it might matter most. Somewhere, the gap began to open. Sometimes it's one clear thing — a loss, a diagnosis, a move, a job that swallowed you. More often it's slower: a season that quietly took more than it gave. There's no wrong story here. Tell me how it went for you — when you first felt the drift, and what it quietly cost you.
