## 1. Voice direction — the biggest input for marketing

We've been actively **thinning the "AI-tell" out of member-facing copy**, and the same discipline should carry straight into marketing voice. The through-line: **declare what something IS; sound like a person, not a brand.** Plain, measured, no hype, no self-aware reassurance.

Three specific cadences we're removing (avoid them in marketing too):

- **The "That's ___ done" hand-off filler.** ("That's What's Your Why? done. Next up…") → just say the next thing. It reads like a wizard, not a guide.
- **The "it's not X, it's Y" negation-contrast** — the self-reassuring marketing tic. Examples we cut: *"never participation," "never a scold," "not a grade — a starting line," "not about a perfect score," "nothing here is a test."* Say the thing plainly instead of defining it by what it isn't. (We also fixed the internal voice *rules* so it stops regenerating.)
- **The generic "honest" AI-tell** — "an honest look," "an honest read," "honestly" as filler → cut.

**BUT — "honest" is a PROTECTED brand word. Lean into it, don't dilute it.** There is exactly one meaning we keep and want to *elevate*: **the member being honest with themselves** — the vulnerable admission, and the self-assessments (the ID Score, the readiness reads). This is central to the whole product (getting a midlife adult to admit, often for the first time and to themselves, what they lost and want back). It's load-bearing, it's emotionally true, and it's a natural hero word for campaigns. Trimming the idle uses everywhere else is specifically so this one lands harder. **When Cowork uses "honest," tie it to self-honesty / being honest with yourself — not to reassurance ("no grade, just an honest read").**

Also holding: **normalize, never praise; reflect, don't extract; the Fade is "a hundred reasonable decisions, not a failing"** (that negation stays — it removes shame, which is the one kind of "not X" we keep).

## 3. The member-experience story

The whole **4Rs conversational arc got a charter-quality copy pass** (Reconnect → Rewire → Rebuild → Reclaim), so the in-product narrative now reads clean and consistent — useful raw material for how we describe "what it's actually like" in marketing.

The shape worth telling:
- **Reconnect** — a short gateway. An honest look at where you are now vs. who you were before the Fade; you recognize the Doors, measure the distance (ID Score), and put words to the life you want back (the Reclaim List).
- **Rewire** (mind) and **Rebuild** (body) — run in parallel, dosed to the member; not a linear pipeline. Catch the lies you tell yourself and build a picture worth moving toward; find the *why* that's actually yours, take stock of your skills, live a week of noticing.
- **Reclaim** — the outcome state. A realization that you're becoming who you set out to be — temporary by design, which is what makes the **Loop** the real product: it fades, you clip back in.

**The Playbook is being positioned as a cornerstone.** Not a scrapbook — **a roll-up of nearly everything valuable a member does or narrates about themselves, and their operating manual for the Loop** ("how did I handle this before? → run that exercise again"). Strong narrative if Cowork wants a "your own playbook, built from your own life" angle.

Positioning guardrail to hold in messaging: G4L is **for people experiencing midlife identity loss — a real Fade.** Not the no-deficit "I just want more" optimizer. The edge is a specific person + a specific problem, told without hype.

- **Prod = v3.2, "the Companion Triptych."** The member surface is architected around a **centered Companion** (the AI guide), flanked by *reflect* (ID Score / Grinta / Badges) and *act* (Momentum, Community, Movement, Reclaim List).
