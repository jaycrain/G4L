### Background — What Reconnect Is

VERBATIM:

```
# Background - What Reconnect Is

Greg Notes: I retained

#### Background for Companion:

Reconnect is the starting line. You cannot Rewire a brain that doesn't know it's been lying to itself. You cannot Rebuild a body you haven't looked at honestly. You cannot Reclaim an identity you haven't excavated. Everything in the G4L framework depends on a member first doing the uncomfortable work of seeing where they actually are or remembering who they actually were.

Reconnect is also the return line. Every cycle through the 4Rs brings a member back here. The second time through, Reconnect looks different — the scars are different, the wisdom is different, the gap between who they are and who they know themselves to be has different dimensions. The Reconnect content must hold up on the first pass and the fifth. It cannot feel like beginner material that members graduate from.

The Design Principle

Every piece of Reconnect content serves one of three functions: helping a member recognize where they are, helping them excavate who they were, or giving them the spark that turns recognition into motion. These three layers — Recognition, Excavation, and Spark — organize everything in this wing.

Why Layers Instead of Stages

These are layers, not stages. A member doesn't complete Recognition and then move to Excavation. They move between layers constantly, sometimes within the same exercise. A member filling out the Identity Distance Questionnaire (Recognition) may suddenly remember a specific moment from their past (Excavation) and feel something shift (Spark). The layers describe the kind of work, not a sequence to follow.

This matters for a member returning to Reconnect on their second or third cycle. The Recognition layer looks different — the crash, the film stress, the slow drift instead of the dramatic collapse. The Excavation layer reaches for a different version of the self. The Spark comes from a different place. The layers hold because they describe universal processes, not specific content.
```

Part A: The Physical Distance

My body feels like it belongs to me — I recognize it, trust it, and feel at home in it.

I have at least one person in my life who sees the real me and tells me the truth.

### Scoring rules / After Completing — VERBATIM

```
After Completing

There is no total score and no pass/fail. Instead, the member is prompted to look at their responses and answer one question:

Where is the biggest gap? Look at where you rated yourself lowest. That's where the Fade has done the most work. Sit with that for a moment before you move on. What you just saw — that's your starting line.

Members are encouraged to save their responses and revisit the questionnaire after 90 days. The distance between the two snapshots becomes their personal measuring stick — data over feelings, evidence that the framework is working.

### The Doors — VERBATIM (7 doors; the bolded label is the Door button text, the paragraph is revealed on click)

The Career Door.

Your work consumed you. The startup, the promotion, the demanding role. Mornings that used to start with movement started with email. Evenings that used to end with stretching ended with spreadsheets. You built something impressive and lost yourself inside it.

The Caregiver Door.

Someone needed you more than you needed yourself. A parent got sick. A spouse needed support. A child needed extra. Your identity got swallowed by the demands of keeping someone else going. There was no time for you. No space for you.

The Empty Nest Door.

The kids left. The role that consumed every morning and every weekend for twenty years just ended. And you stood somewhere on a Tuesday afternoon and realized you didn't know what to do with yourself when nobody needed you to do anything.

The Body Door.

Your body changed. The knees, the back, the shoulder that doesn't rotate like it used to. The physical identity you'd carried since high school started to feel like a memory instead of a reality. You didn't quit your sport. Your body quit it for you.

The Relationship Door.

A relationship ended or changed so fundamentally that the person you were inside it doesn't exist anymore. Divorce, estrangement, the slow erosion of a marriage. Your identity as a partner came unmoored.

The Social Door.

Your friends faded. Moved away. Got busy. Stopped calling. The social circle that used to hold you up contracted until you realized you hadn't had a real conversation with someone outside your family in months.

The Autopilot Door.

The most common one. The one nobody talks about because it's not dramatic enough for a diagnosis. Decades of routine without reflection. Work, eat, sleep, repeat. You didn't choose to lose yourself. ### Step 1 — Drift Quiz

Intro VERBATIM:

```
### Step 1. Drift Quiz

We call this activity the 'Drift Quiz' The Fade you explored in the last activity doesn't announce itself. It shows up as a thousand small decisions that each feel reasonable. This quiz helps you catch the pattern. There are no wrong answers. This is a mirror so reflect on the items a bit and see what you learn through the process.

Items — VERBATIM, each with its exact 4-option (a/b/c/d) response set and its Drift indicator scoring rule:

```
Questions

Q1. The last time you skipped something physical (a walk, a workout, a ride), the reason was:

a) An injury or illness that genuinely prevented it

b) A real scheduling conflict I couldn't work around

c) I was tired, busy, or just didn't feel like it — and it felt like a perfectly reasonable call at the time

d) I can't remember the last time I had something physical to skip

Drift indicator: c and d. C is the Fade in action — the reasonable decision. D means the default already flipped.

Q2. When someone asks how you're doing, what do you typically do?

a) I tell them the truth, good or bad

b) "Good" or "Busy" — the automatic response

c) "I'm alright" — and I mostly believe it

d) I change the subject

Drift indicator: b, c, and d. B is autopilot. C is the disinformation campaign. D is avoidance.

Q3. Think about the last month. How many times did you do something purely for yourself — something that had nothing to do with work, family, or obligation?

a) Multiple times a week

b) A few times

c) Once, maybe

d) I genuinely cannot think of one

Drift indicator: c and d. The Fade erases personal time so gradually you don't notice it's gone.

Q4. The last time you looked at yourself in a full-length mirror, your honest first reaction was:

a) Recognition — that's me

b) Neutral — I don't really think about it

c) Avoidance — I don't spend time looking

d) I don't recognize the person I'm looking at

Drift indicator: c and d. Avoidance is the disinformation campaign's strongest tool.

Q5. If your twelve-year-old self could see your average Tuesday, they would think:

a) That looks like a life I'd want

b) That looks responsible and sensible

c) Where's the fun?

d) Who is that person?

Drift indicator: c and d. C is the beginning of the distance. D is the full Fade.

Q6. When was the last time you felt genuinely excited about something coming up in your life — not relieved, not obligated, but actually looking forward to it?

a) This week

b) This month

c) I'd have to think about it

d) I honestly can't remember

Drift indicator: c and d. The Fade doesn't just take your body. It takes your anticipation.
```

Scoring / After Completing — VERBATIM:

```
After Completing

The quiz does not produce a score. Instead, the member sees a brief reflection:

Read to Member; Look at where you landed. If you saw drift indicators in more than half your answers, the Fade has been running quietly in the background of your life. That's not a judgment. It's what happened to Jay. It's what happens to almost every midlifer who built a life for everyone else and forgot to keep building themselves. The fact that you're here, looking at it honestly, means the Reconnect has already begun.

Read to Member: The next step is how you move forward to what is possible
```

Scoring rule restated: no score is produced. The only threshold in the instrument is **"drift indicators in more than half your answers"** (i.e. > 3 of 6), which triggers the reflection copy above. Per-item drift keys: Q1 c/d · Q2 b/c/d · Q3 c/d · Q4 c/d · Q5 c/d · Q6 c/d.

### Step 2 — Legacy Letter

VERBATIM, in full:

```
### Step 2 Legacy Letter

This exercise asks you to write a letter — not about where you've been, but about where you're going. Who are you becoming? And what do you want the next chapter to stand for?

Suggestions for the Letter

Write a letter to yourself, dated one year from today. Address it to the version of you that has continued the work — the person who stayed in the circle. Answer these prompts in whatever order feels right:

What does a Tuesday look like for you one year from now? Not the highlight reel — the ordinary day. How does it start? How does it end? Who is in it?

What adventure have you completed that you haven't started yet today?

What relationship has deepened because you kept showing up?

What have you given back? Who have you brought into the circle?

What does the measuring stick say? What's the data point that proves the next year was real?

What is your Unfinished Business? (There should always be Unfinished Business. That's the point.)

After Completing

The Legacy Letter is private by default. Members are invited — never pressured — to share one sentence from it in The Spark. Even a single line from a Legacy Letter ("In a year, I want to be the person who planned the trip instead of thinking about it") creates a powerful community moment.

Members are encouraged to save their letter and open it one year later. The distance between what they wrote and where they actually are becomes the most personal measuring stick in the entire program. If they've exceeded the letter, that's a Success Story. If they've drifted from it, that's the signal to start the cycle again. Either way, the letter served its purpose.
```
