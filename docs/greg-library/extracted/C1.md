## Requirements

### [ID] C1-01
- **Type:** pacing
- **Requirement:** C1 exists to help the Member revisit and refine their ReClaim List after they have moved through ReConnect, ReWire and ReBuild.
- **Verbatim:** "The Readiness activity is designed to help a person revisit and refine their ReClaim List after moving through the earlier work of ReConnect, ReWire, and ReBuild."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** C1 is reachable only inside ReClaim, after the three prior modules, and its opening screen names the ReClaim List as the object of the activity.

### [ID] C1-02
- **Type:** revisit
- **Requirement:** The activity must return the Member to their own earlier list, but as a changed person — the list is re-read through the self they are now.
- **Verbatim:** "C1 helps the Member return to that list through a changed self."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** The opening frame explicitly tells the Member they are re-reading the list as someone who knows more, and the original list is on screen when it says so.

### [ID] C1-03
- **Type:** revisit
- **Requirement:** The original ReClaim List must not be treated as fixed; reflection is expected to change how the Member sees their own priorities.
- **Verbatim:** "A distinctive strength of this activity is that it does not treat the original ReClaim List as fixed. Instead, it assumes that reflection should change the way a person sees their own priorities."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** Every item on the loaded list is editable/removable/reorderable by the Member; nothing is read-only.

### [ID] C1-04
- **Type:** revisit
- **Requirement:** The design must accommodate three outcomes per item — still central, no longer fitting, and brand-new priorities emerging.
- **Verbatim:** "Some original items may still feel central. Others may now seem too vague, borrowed, performative, unrealistic, or disconnected from what the person actually values. New priorities may also emerge."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** The activity supports keeping an item, demoting/removing an item, and adding an item that was not on the original list.

### [ID] C1-05
- **Type:** posture
- **Requirement:** The reassessment must be made explicit rather than left implicit.
- **Verbatim:** "C1 gains strength from making that reassessment explicit rather than leaving it implicit."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** The Member is asked, in words, to re-evaluate the list — the revision is a named step, not a side effect of another activity.

### [ID] C1-06
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** C1 must directly examine which goals are only partly the Member's own — inherited from family, culture, comparison, fear, or an earlier self.
- **Verbatim:** "They may inherit aspirations from family, culture, comparison, fear, or an earlier version of themselves. Those goals can still produce effort, but they often do not sustain meaningful engagement over time... C1 helps the Member examine that issue directly by asking which goals still matter most, which now feel less important, and which have become more concrete because of what they have learned."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** The conversation contains an explicit ownership/borrowedness question, and its answer is stored per item.

### [ID] C1-07
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** C1 must combine two processes — reflection on progress and guided goal refinement — because either alone is insufficient.
- **Verbatim:** "C1 also works well because it combines two important processes: reflection on progress and guided goal refinement... This pairing is valuable because reflection alone can remain abstract, while goal revision alone can become mechanical."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** The activity has both a reflect-on-what-changed segment and a change-the-list segment; neither is skippable by design.

### [ID] C1-08

## In-app summary (verbatim)

### In-app summary: C1 "Readiness"

This activity helps you revisit your ReClaim List after everything you have learned in the earlier stages of the program. That matters because the goals you name at the beginning of a journey are not always the ones that remain most meaningful once you understand yourself more clearly. After reflecting on identity, noticing patterns in your self-talk and focus, and learning more about your habits and health decisions, you may see your priorities differently. Some goals may now feel more real, more urgent, or more possible. Others may feel too vague, borrowed, or less important than they once did. Research in this area suggests that goals are more likely to hold when they feel personally meaningful and aligned with who you are and who you are becoming. By doing this activity, you give yourself a chance to refine your priorities so the next steps in ReClaim are grounded in clearer ownership, stronger relevance, and deeper readiness.

## Contradictions

1. **Six stages vs. eight stages.** The Engineering Memo's "Conversation sequencing logic" enumerates **eight** numbered stages (Engagement, Enduring goals, De-prioritized goals, Ownership and vagueness check, Concretization, Emergent priorities, Reordering, Summary and confirmation), but its own Implementation checklist says "**Six-stage** conversation sequence implemented with stage tracking." The six correspond to the six core questions (stages 2–7); Engagement and Summary/confirmation are the two extra bookends. Resolution needed: is stage tracking over 6 or 8?

2. **Question 4's phrasing vs. its heading.** The Companion Memo's OARS list gives the fourth core question as "Which now feel more concrete?" while the detailed section heads it "Which now feel more concrete because of what I've learned?" and gives the member-facing phrasing as "Which goals feel clearer or more tangible now than they did at the beginning?" — three variants of the same question.

3. **"Not longer or more ambitious" vs. "sometimes more ambitious."** The Companion Memo says refinement is "making the list sharper and more owned, **not longer or more ambitious**." The Science Check's closing nuance says refinement "Other times... makes a goal **more ambitious** because it now feels more authentic and more worth the effort." The Companion Memo is constraining the *Companion's* pushing; the Science Check is describing a legitimate *Member* outcome — but the wording collides and needs a stated resolution so the Companion does not suppress a Member who enlarges a goal.

4. **Habit anchoring: "not primarily" vs. "save for B3/C3."** The Companion Memo permits a "light touch" habit remark in C1 but the operating rules say "Save habit anchoring for assets where it is the primary function." The Engineering Memo's constraints and checklist mention habit anchoring nowhere at all — so the light-touch allowance has no engineering expression.

5. **Prior-module completion: hard gate vs. graceful fallback.** The Engineering Memo requires "the system has confirmed completion of prior ReConnect, ReWire, and ReBuild module work" as a pivot trigger, but immediately allows "(or, if skipped, has a graceful fallback)" — so it is simultaneously a precondition and not one. The Science Check assumes prior completion throughout ("it assumes growth has already happened").

## Anything unclear

- **What "the original ReClaim List" means in our build.** Greg says "from onboarding or prior capture." Our list is member-editable from the Companion rail at any time, so by C1 there may be no single "original" — the stored list may already have drifted. Greg does not say whether C1 loads the *onboarding-frozen* list or the *current* list, and the then-vs-now requirement (C1-62, C1-91) depends entirely on which. This is the single biggest open question.
- **How `current_status` values interact.** The enum is still-important / less-important / revised / removed / uncertain — but "revised" and "less-important" are not mutually exclusive in practice, and Greg does not say whether the field is single-valued.
- **Where `ownership_flag` and `clarity_flag` come from.** They are Member-facing judgements elicited in stages 4 and 5, but Greg does not say whether the Member sets them explicitly (a tap) or the Companion infers them from the conversation. Inference would sit uneasily with C1-61 ("never rank goals by inferred importance") and with the "member words outrank the model guess" rule.
