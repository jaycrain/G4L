## In-app summary (verbatim)

**In-app summary: B1 "What Is Your Why?"**

This activity helps you get clearer on why taking care of your body really matters to you. That is important because change is much harder to sustain when it is driven only by guilt, pressure, or the feeling that you "should" do better. Those reasons can get you started, but they often do not carry you very far. This exercise helps you look underneath those surface pressures and identify the motives that feel more personal, meaningful, and lasting - the reasons connected to your energy, your health, your freedom, your relationships, and the kind of life you want to live. Research in this area suggests that people are more likely to stay engaged when their motivation feels more deeply their own. By doing this activity, you give yourself a stronger reason to keep showing up for movement, eating well, and caring for yourself over time.

## Contradictions

1. **Five stages vs. five MI phases — the phase/stage maps do not line up.** The Companion Memo's MI phases are Engagement → Focus → Evocation → Informing → Consolidation, and the Engineering Memo repeats that as `mi_phase_progression`. But the Engineering Memo's *conversation sequencing* is Engagement → Elicitation (activity) → Elicitation (eating) → Didactic informing → Consolidation, and the implementation checklist names that second list as "the five-stage conversation sequence." "Focus" has no corresponding stage, and the single "Evocation" phase spans two stages. Unresolved: does the engine track five phases, five stages, or both in parallel?

2. **Didactic informing as a fixed stage vs. an interleaved move.** The Engineering Memo makes didactic informing Stage 4, sitting between eating elicitation and consolidation, and says "Return to elicitation after sharing" — i.e. the sequence loops back after a stage that is defined as coming after elicitation. The Companion Memo instead treats didactic sharing as a move that is interleaved ("Share briefly, then return to elicitation"; "Always return to the Member's own reflection"). Unresolved: is informing a discrete stage or an interruptible move available in any turn?

3. **"Never force all stages" vs. the acceptance checklist requiring the five-stage sequence and separate domain capture.** Stage management says "never force all stages if the Member is ready to consolidate early," but the implementation checklist requires "Five-stage conversation sequence implemented" and "Activity and eating motivations captured separately." A member who consolidates after the activity stage produces an empty `eating_motivations` array. Unresolved: is such a session complete, and does it still store a valid baseline?

4. **Ownership flags vs. "never grade the baseline."** The data model requires every captured motivation to carry an ownership flag of intrinsic / extrinsic / mixed / uncertain, and `motivational_profile` is a composite characterization. Every other rule in the memos forbids evaluating, ranking, or presenting one profile as better — yet the flag is precisely the intrinsic-vs-extrinsic axis the didactic content describes as differing in durability. Unresolved: who assigns the flag (member or model), and whether the flag is ever shown to the member.

5. **"Prior ReConnect and ReWire module work is completed" as a hard trigger condition vs. "or available as context."** Both memos hedge with "completed **or** available as context," but the Engineering Memo also lists it as one of three conditions that must **all** be true for the pivot to fire. Unresolved: whether an incomplete ReWire blocks B1 entry, degrades it, or is simply passed through empty.

## Anything unclear

1. **There is no instrument.** Despite repeated references to "the motivational self-assessment," "any structured assessment," and "structured interaction ... plus visible baseline state," no scale, item set, anchors, or response options are specified anywhere in the three documents.
