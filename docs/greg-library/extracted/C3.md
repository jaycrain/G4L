## Requirements

### [ID] C3-01
- **Type:** didactic
- **Requirement:** C3 is self-monitoring of daily patterns that constitute a life of wellness, not tracking of discrete health behaviors in isolation.
- **Verbatim:** "The Quality Days activity is designed to help a person self-monitor the daily patterns that constitute a life of wellness rather than only tracking discrete health behaviors in isolation."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** The C3 opening frame describes tracking whole days, and the check-in asks about the day, not about a single logged behavior (steps, meals, minutes).

### [ID] C3-02
- **Type:** didactic
- **Requirement:** C3 is the program's clearest translation of the CFW process-product principle: do the process, the product follows to the extent possible for you.
- **Verbatim:** "it represents the program's clearest translation of a core principle from Concepts of Fitness and Wellness: if you do the process, the product follows to the extent possible for you."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** The phrase "to the extent possible for you" (or an equivalent hedge) appears in the C3 framing/closing copy; no copy asserts wellness as a guaranteed result.

### [ID] C3-03
- **Type:** threading
- **Requirement:** C3 extends B3's process-product logic upward: in B3 the process was physical activity and healthy eating for fitness/health; in C3 the process is self-monitoring Quality Days and the product is wellness.
- **Verbatim:** "In the B3 activity, that principle was applied to physical activity and healthy eating as the process behind fitness and health. In C3, the same principle is extended upward: self-monitoring Quality Days is the process, and wellness is the product."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** C3 copy explicitly names the B3 parallel; the mapping process=tracking Quality Days / product=wellness is stated in the closing.

## In-app summary (verbatim)

> This activity helps you monitor something more than a single habit. It helps you track Quality Days - days that reflect the kind of life you are building. That matters because wellness does not arrive all at once. It grows out of daily patterns, just as fitness grows out of regular physical activity. In this program, healthy lifestyles are the process, and wellness is the product. Tracking Quality Days helps you notice which behaviors, choices, and conditions make a day feel like a good day, so that quality becomes something you can repeat and build on. Research in this area suggests that self-monitoring a target process increases awareness and consistency, which over time supports the outcomes you are aiming for. By doing this activity, you practice the same kind of attention that builds fitness and health, but applied to the larger goal of well-being, outlook, and life satisfaction.

## Contradictions

1. **One question per check-in vs. the two-question check-in shape.** The Engineering Memo's checklist says "One major question per check-in enforced" and the Companion Memo says "One major question per turn during check-ins," but both memos also specify a check-in that asks two things: Engineering Stage 5 says "Ask what was in the day that felt like quality" *and* "Ask what got in the way," and the Companion Memo's model flow shows the same pair. Either "what got in the way" is a follow-up turn rather than part of the same turn, or the one-question rule is scoped to turns and not to the whole check-in. Needs a ruling before the check-in is built.

2. **Streak milestones (3, 5, 7) vs. the memo's own example affirmations.** The Engineering Memo specifies "Generate streak affirmations at milestones (3, 5, 7 days)," but the Companion Memo's worked examples affirm at four days ("You've checked in four days in a row — that's a real rhythm") and at five ("You've checked in five days in a row"). Four is not a milestone under the engineering rule.

3. **Monitoring period length — "a week" vs. an open-ended period.** The Companion Memo commits the member to "the next week" and the Engineering Memo says the monitoring period is "nominally one week," but the Companion Memo also says to use summaries "at the end of each week or at key transitions during the monitoring period" and Engineering says summaries fire "at the end of each week or cluster of days" — both implying the period may run longer than one week. Whether the week is a fixed window or a first cycle is not settled.

4. **Dashboard visibility vs. no-count-affirmation.** The Companion Memo requires that "The Member's tracked Quality Days should be visible in the dashboard" and that the member can "see your week," while also banning any affirmation or comment on "the number of Quality Days." Showing a week of yes/partial/no entries inherently displays a count. The docs do not say how to render the week without making the count the salient figure.

## Anything unclear

- **Shared machinery with B3 is asserted conceptually but never specified.** Both memos require the B3 parallel be *referenced* in copy, and Engineering requires "B3 monitoring experience available as a parallel reference," but no doc says whether C3 should reuse B3's self-monitoring components, streak logic, reminder plumbing, or data model — only that the logic is analogous. Greg says the parallel "is not metaphorical," which reads as a claim about behavior-change science, not an instruction to share code.
- **How the member's 2-3 components relate to the daily entry.** `definition_components` holds 2-3 items and the entry holds a single `quality_present` (yes/partial/no) plus free text. It is not stated whether the member rates each component per day, or only the day as a whole. "Partial" implies component-level partiality but no component-level field exists.
- **Who initiates the daily check-in.** The docs specify a member-chosen cue, optional reminders anchored to that cue, and "let the Member decide when to check in" — but also describe the Companion as opening the check-in ("Let's take a look at today"). Whether the reminder is the initiator, whether the Companion proactively opens a thread each day, or whether the member must come to it, is not resolved. This matters for the outreach/nudge surface.
- **"Days_elapsed" is tracked but never used.** The Engineering Memo lists `days_elapsed` under stage management but it appears in no output field, UI requirement, or affirmation rule.
- **What "backup for missed days" means concretely.** Stage 4 requires "Identify backup for missed days" but no doc describes what a backup is — a second reminder, a catch-up entry for yesterday, a lowered bar. Whether retroactive entries are permitted at all is unspecified.
- **The C3 "close" and its relationship to the program's Loop.** Stage 8 says to connect to "continued ReClaim work, future cycles," and the Science Check frames Quality Days as the reciprocal loop, but no doc states whether completing C3 triggers, contributes to, or is independent of the ReClaim→ReConnect Loop.
- **Whether "Quality Days" is member-facing branded vocabulary.** It is capitalized throughout all three docs and appears in the in-app summary, so it appears to be a real name, but it is not in the current G4L locked vocabulary list.
