## Requirements

### [ID] R1-01
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** R1 is the first gated asset of ReConnect and sits in the Recognition layer — the awareness step of the Aware → Prepare → Engage progression.
- **Verbatim:** "This memo guides the AI Companion in how to support Members during the Identity Distance Questionnaire (IDQ), the first gated asset of ReConnect. R1 is the Recognition layer and the awareness step in the Aware → Prepare → Engage progression of ReConnect."
- **Source:** Companion Memo
- **Testable as:** R1 is the first gated asset presented in the ReConnect phase; asset metadata tags it as the Recognition/awareness step.

### [ID] R1-02
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** The instrument asks the member to rate themselves across a set of life and self domains, comparing present self to their fuller/more authentic remembered self.
- **Verbatim:** "It asks the member to rate, across a set of life and self domains, where they feel they actually are now compared with the version of themselves they recognize as their fuller or more authentic self."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** Each rating prompt explicitly frames the comparison as now-vs-remembered-fuller-self, not an absolute judgment.

- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** The rating domains are exactly six, in this sequence: identity, body, energy, relationships, work, outlook.
- **Verbatim:** "For each domain (identity, body, energy, relationships, work, outlook), in sequence:"
- **Source:** Engineering Memo (also Companion Memo: "The focus is set by the rating domains: identity, body, energy, relationships, work, outlook.")

### [ID] R1-04
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** The rating scale is 1 to 5, anchored 1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree, and the scale is presented with each domain.

### [ID] R1-05
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** The member must be instructed to rate based on their current perspective, not what they want to be true.
- **Verbatim:** "The framing is explicit: rate each statement based on your current perspective (not what you want them to be)."
- **Source:** Companion Memo (echoed in Engineering Memo: "Set the contract: rate based on your current perspective, not what you want to be true")
- **Testable as:** The opening frame contains the "current perspective, not what you want to be true" contract before any rating is collected.

### [ID] R1-06
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** Rating must be domain-by-domain rather than a single global judgment, because the pattern of highs and lows matters more than any single score.
- **Verbatim:** "By asking the member to rate themselves across multiple domains rather than in a single global judgment, it reduces the tendency to summarize the self too quickly as either \"fine\" or \"broken.\" The pattern of highs and lows that emerges is more useful than any single score, because it shows where the Fade has done the most work and where the member still feels relatively intact."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** No single composite verdict is shown; the surfaced output is a per-domain profile.

### [ID] R1-07
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** The comparison reference must be the member's own remembered self, never a population norm, clinical threshold, or cultural ideal.
- **Verbatim:** "The IDQ does not ask the member to measure themselves against a population norm, a clinical threshold, or an idealized image taken from culture. It asks them to compare their present self with the version of themselves they actually recognize as more whole, more alive, more \"them.\""
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** No normative comparison, percentile, or benchmark appears anywhere in R1 copy or output.

## In-app summary (verbatim)

> In-app summary: R1 "Identity Distance Questionnaire"
>
> This activity helps you see, with honesty, how far you have drifted from the person you used to be. That matters because the rest of the G4L journey depends on first knowing where you actually are — you cannot rebuild a body you have not looked at honestly, and you cannot reclaim an identity you have not excavated. This exercise asks you to rate yourself across several areas of life, comparing where you feel you are now with the version of yourself you recognize as more whole, more alive, more "you." The point is not to judge yourself harshly but to see the gap clearly, because a gap you can name is a gap you can work to close. Research in this area suggests that honest self-comparison — looking at the present self against a personally meaningful reference — can strengthen motivation and sharpen the work that follows. By doing this activity, you give yourself a clearer starting line. You can retake this questionnaire later, and the distance between your snapshots over time becomes your personal measuring stick — evidence that the framework is working, or an honest signal about where the Fade is still active.

## Contradictions

1. **Revisit cadence vs. the frozen G4L data contract.** All three Greg docs specify a **90-day / quarterly** retake ("retake the questionnaire after roughly 90 days", "You can retake this in 90 days", "Quarterly revisit reminder"). CLAUDE.md's frozen IDQ data contract states the IDQ is "**Retaken every 60 days**." Greg's docs are internally consistent with each other; the conflict is Greg-vs-platform-contract, not doc-vs-doc.

2. **Number of items / domains vs. the frozen IDQ schema.** Greg specifies **six domains** — identity, body, energy, relationships, work, outlook. CLAUDE.md's frozen contract specifies "**24 items × 4 dimensions (Physical, Self, Social, Outlook)**." Only "Outlook" appears in both. Again Greg-internal consistency; conflict is with the platform contract.

3. **Scale anchors vs. the comparison frame.** The Companion Memo specifies the scale as "1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree" (an agreement scale implying rated *statements*), while the Science Check and the rating prompts describe rating a *domain* on distance from the remembered self ("When you think about your body right now, where would you rate yourself?"). Agreement anchors do not fit a bare domain prompt; one of the two framings is imprecise.

4. **Whether R1 produces a score.** The Engineering Memo says "The intended output is not a score or a verdict" and forbids computing a score presented as a verdict, while the Science Check refers to "any single score" and "the pattern of highs and lows … more useful than any single score" — and the platform contract has R1 producing the baseline **ID Score**. Greg's prohibition is on *presenting* a computed score as a verdict; whether an ID Score may be computed at all from R1 is not resolved by these docs.

## Anything unclear

1. **What the member actually rates.** The docs alternate between "rate each statement" (Companion Memo, autonomy section) and "rate the domain" (everywhere else). Whether each of the six domains contains multiple statement-level items, or is a single 1–5 rating, is never stated.

2. **Direction of the scale.** "Where did you rate yourself lowest? That's where the Fade has done the most work" implies **low = large gap**. But with agree/disagree anchors, the polarity depends entirely on the wording of the unstated statements. The largest_gap_domain derivation ("The domain where the Member rated themselves lowest") is only correct under one polarity, and the item wording is not supplied.

3. **The member-facing opening frame text.** Both memos say to "present the member-facing frame" / "the member-facing script provides the framing," but neither doc contains that script. It is referenced as existing elsewhere.

4. **Silence thresholds.** "After a second silence, offer the Member the option to skip" — no duration is given for what counts as a silence, and R1 is a conversational text surface where "silence" needs an operational definition.

5. **Elevation to coach mode.** The Companion Memo allows elevating to coach "if the Member signals that they want to act on what they are noticing," but the Engineering Memo says companion_mode is "guide throughout" and forbids pushing toward action planning. What the Companion actually does with an in-session act-signal is unspecified.

6. **"Book Quiz" as a trigger.** Completion of "the onboarding Book Quiz" is a hard trigger condition in both memos, but the Book Quiz is not otherwise described here, and no mapping to the platform's existing onboarding is given.

7. **Where the ~90-day reminder fires from.** UX requirement 9 says "the Member is told they can retake this in 90 days," which is a statement at closure. Whether an actual scheduled reminder/notification mechanic is required — or only the in-closure statement — is not specified.
