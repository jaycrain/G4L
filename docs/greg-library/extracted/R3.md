## Requirements

### [ID] R3-01
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** R3 is one activity with two parts, run in sequence: the Drift Quiz first, then the Legacy Letter.
- **Verbatim:** "The activity has two complementary parts. The Drift Quiz is a mirror, not a test. ... The Legacy Letter then turns attention forward."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** Entering R3 runs Part 1 (Drift Quiz) to completion before Part 2 (Legacy Letter) is offered; there is no path that starts at the letter.

### [ID] R3-02
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** The two parts must never be offered separately or independently.
- **Verbatim:** "The two parts are deliberately paired rather than offered separately. Vision without truth becomes fantasy, and truth without vision becomes discouragement."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** No route/entry point exposes the Legacy Letter or the Drift Quiz as a standalone asset.

## In-app summary (verbatim)

> In-app summary: R3 "Drift Quiz and Legacy Letter"
>
> This activity helps you do two important things at once: see your drift more clearly and remember where you want to go. The first part, the Drift Quiz, helps you notice how the Fade can show up in small, everyday choices that slowly pull you away from the life, energy, and identity you want. It is a mirror, not a test — there are no wrong answers, only patterns to see. The second part, the Legacy Letter, helps you look forward and put words to the person you want to become and what you want the next chapter of your life to stand for. That matters because change is easier to sustain when you are not just reacting to what feels wrong, but also moving toward something that feels deeply yours. Research in this area suggests that people are more motivated when they can clearly imagine their future self and feel connected to it, and that writing down your intentions makes them clearer and more lasting. By working through this activity, you give yourself both a more honest mirror and a more meaningful direction — and you create a letter you can come back to later, as a marker of where you were aiming when you started.

## Contradictions

1. **Closure is five steps or four steps.** The Companion Memo specifies a five-step closure ("Closure sequence: 1. Read back ... 2. Identify the most real part ... 3. Connect to R1 and R2 ... 4. Save as a reference ... 5. Foreshadow lightly" and "The Companion guides the five-step closure sequence"). The Engineering Memo collapses save + foreshadow into one and repeatedly calls it four ("Closure sequence — guided four-step closure (read back, identify most real part, connect to R1/R2, save and foreshadow)"; "Closure: medium — enough to guide the four-step sequence"; checklist item 5 "Closure sequence implements the four-step flow"). The moves are the same; only the count differs.

2. **Engineering Stage numbering vs. the closure count.** The Engineering Memo's own stage list gives closure four stages (Stage 5 read back, Stage 6 anchor, Stage 7 connect, Stage 8 save and foreshadow) but the Companion Memo's Stage-8 equivalent is two distinct member-facing moves with distinct copy ("Your letter is saved for you. You can come back to it anytime." and "In W2, we'll build on this..."). Implementers must decide whether "save" gets its own member-facing beat.

3. **Community share placement.** Both memos say the Legacy Letter can optionally be shared in the Spark space, but neither the Companion Memo's five-step closure nor the Engineering Memo's Stage 5–8 closure includes a share step, and there is no output field for share state in the data model. The invitation is required but has no specified moment or capture.

## Anything unclear

1. **The number and content of Drift Quiz prompts is never specified.** All three docs describe the per-prompt loop, sequencing, skipping, and tagging, but no doc gives the item count, the item text, or the pattern taxonomy that recognitions are "tagged by." The Legacy Letter's five prompts are given verbatim; the Drift Quiz's are not. This is the single largest gap for build.
2. **No scale or anchors exist for the Drift Quiz.** It is called a "quiz" and a "mirror," and prompts ask the member "to recognize whether it shows up in their life" — but no response scale, no anchors, and explicitly no score. Whether the response is binary recognition, free text, or both is unstated; `drift_recognitions` stores `{pattern, member_recognition, member_language}`, which implies a structured recognition value plus verbatim text, but the recognition value's type is undefined.
3. **How the letter is physically produced is ambiguous.** The Engineering Memo requires a "Legacy Letter writing interface" and says "Capture the letter text as the Member writes it," while the flow is five conversational prompts with the Companion drawing the member from adjectives into scenes. Whether `legacy_letter_text` is a single member-typed document, or an assembly of the five prompt answers, is not stated — and the memos forbid the system supplying content, so an auto-assembled letter may violate autonomy.
4. **Revisability has no mechanism.** The letter is "revisable" and the member decides "whether to revise it later," but no doc specifies where revision happens, whether prior versions are retained, or whether W2 reads the original or the latest version.
5. **Duration is unstated.** R3 is "a single session" but no expected time-on-asset, turn budget, or guidance on whether a member may pause and resume across days is given (only that disengagement "gracefully suspends with partial capture retained").
6. **`drift_prompts_completed` / `drift_prompts_skipped` imply a fixed, enumerable prompt set** — which reinforces item 1: the set exists in Greg's model but is not written down in any of the three docs.
7. **"Spark space" is named as the community surface** for optional letter sharing, but no doc defines whether that is an existing Community space, a new one, or what moderation/consent applies to sharing a Legacy Letter.
8. **`member_correction` captures corrections to the closure summary**, but no doc says what happens to the letter or the anchor element when the member corrects the summary — whether the correction overwrites, appends, or is stored alongside.
