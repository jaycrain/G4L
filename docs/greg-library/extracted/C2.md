## Requirements

### [ID] C2-01
- **Type:** didactic
- **Requirement:** C2's purpose is to help the member recognize how their life can expand as they move against the contraction of the Fade.
- **Verbatim:** "The Bigger World Audit activity is designed to help a person recognize how their life can expand as they begin to move against the contraction of the Fade."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** The C2 opening frame names expansion-against-contraction as the subject of the activity, not habits or goals.

### [ID] C2-02
- **Type:** didactic
- **Requirement:** The framing must state that people lose *range*, not only habits, health, or confidence — their world becomes narrower, more repetitive, more defensive, more organized around avoidance.
- **Verbatim:** "many people do not only lose habits, health, or confidence over time - they also lose range. Their world becomes narrower, more repetitive, more defensive, and more organized around avoidance, limitation, or survival."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** Member-facing C2 copy contains the loss-of-range framing, not only a deficit/habit framing.

### [ID] C2-03
- **Type:** didactic
- **Requirement:** C2 must surface the opposite possibility — that change reopens options, widens participation, restores curiosity, increases engagement.
- **Verbatim:** "C2 helps the Member notice the opposite possibility: that meaningful change can reopen options, widen participation, restore curiosity, and increase engagement with life."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** The activity asks about reopened options / widened participation / restored curiosity / increased engagement.

### [ID] C2-04
- **Type:** threading
- **Requirement:** C2 sits after ReConnect, ReWire and ReBuild and changes the question the member is asked — from "what is wrong" to "what becomes possible if I keep going."
- **Verbatim:** "By the time the Member reaches ReClaim, they are in a better position to ask a different question: not only \"What is wrong?\" or \"What do I need to fix?\" but \"What becomes possible if I keep going?\" C2 helps shift attention toward that wider horizon."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** C2's opening explicitly contrasts the new question against the earlier fix-what's-wrong questions.

### [ID] C2-05
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** The audit asks the member to evaluate the *size and shape of their world*, in a lived/ecological form, not symptoms, habits or isolated goals.
- **Verbatim:** "Instead of focusing only on symptoms, habits, or isolated goals, it asks the Member to evaluate the size and shape of their world."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** No C2 question is a habit-adherence or goal-progress question; all are about the shape of life.

### [ID] C2-06
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** The Science Check names the audit's content as: what they are willing to try, where they are willing to go, how open they are to challenge, how much energy they bring to relationships, whether they can imagine new chapters, and whether life feels broader or smaller than it once did.
- **Verbatim:** "That may include what they are willing to try, where they are willing to go, how open they are to challenge, how much energy they bring to relationships, whether they can imagine new chapters, and whether life feels broader or smaller than it once did."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** Each of these six probes is reachable somewhere in the C2 dialogue or dimension set.

### [ID] C2-07
- **Type:** measurement
- **Requirement:** Well-being here is read as increased participation, flexibility, vitality and opportunity — not reduced distress.
- **Verbatim:** "This is scientifically meaningful because well-being is often reflected not just in reduced distress, but in increased participation, flexibility, vitality, and opportunity."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** C2 outputs contain no distress/symptom measure; the recorded signal is participation and possibility.

### [ID] C2-08

- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** The activity has an explicit place for naming what has narrowed, not only what has opened.

## In-app summary (verbatim)

> This activity helps you look at whether your world is getting bigger. That matters because people do not only struggle through pain or bad habits - they also often begin living in a smaller and smaller world. They pull back, stop exploring, stop trying, and stop imagining what might still be possible. This exercise helps you notice the opposite pattern. It asks whether your life is becoming more open, more active, more connected, and more full of possibility. Research in this area suggests that people tend to do better when they can stay engaged with what matters, remain open to challenge, and move toward a life that feels broader rather than narrower. By doing this activity, you gain a clearer sense of whether your effort is helping you reclaim not just progress, but range, participation, and possibility.

## The dimensions of expansion (exact wording)

The canonical set is **five**, stated identically in the Companion Memo and the Engineering Memo:

1. **movement**
2. **connection**
3. **curiosity**
4. **willingness**
5. **future possibility** (stored as `future-possibility`; the data model also permits `other`)

- Companion Memo, Phase 2 Focus: "Direct attention to the dimensions of expansion the audit examines: movement, connection, curiosity, willingness, future possibility."
- Companion Memo, pivot rationale: "The Member is being asked to evaluate whether their life is becoming broader — across movement, connection, curiosity, willingness, and future possibility."
- Engineering Memo, UX requirements: "Present the dimensions of expansion clearly so the Member knows what they're assessing (movement, connection, curiosity, willingness, future possibility)"
- Engineering Memo, data model: "dimension | movement / connection / curiosity / willingness / future-possibility / other"

Each dimension carries the four-value member assessment: **expanding / mixed / still-narrow / uncertain** (the Engineering Memo's autonomy section words the same set as "expanded, mixed, still narrow, or uncertain").

## Contradictions

1. **Dimension naming — the Science Check never uses the canonical five.** The Science Check gives two *different*, longer, informal lists: (a) "what they are willing to try, where they are willing to go, how open they are to challenge, how much energy they bring to relationships, whether they can imagine new chapters, and whether life feels broader or smaller than it once did" (six probes), and (b) in scaffold 3, "relationships, health, confidence, interest, and future possibility" (five, but only *future possibility* overlaps with the canonical set; **health** and **confidence** appear nowhere in the two memos' dimension set). The memos are internally consistent with each other; the Science Check is not aligned with them.

2. **Assessment-value wording drifts within the Engineering Memo.** Autonomy section: "expanded, mixed, still narrow, or uncertain". Data model and UX/checklist: "expanding / mixed / still-narrow / uncertain". Past participle vs present participle for the first value.

3. **Planning stage vs six-stage sequence.** The Companion Memo names MI Phase 4 Planning ("Light in C2") and the Engineering Memo's `mi_phase_progression` ends in `planning`, but the Engineering Memo's six-stage conversation sequence has no planning stage — Stage 5 is prior-work connection and Stage 6 is summary/closing. Nothing in the sequence implements the light-planning move ("help them consider how to reinforce those conditions").

4. **Habit anchoring has no engineering home.** The Companion Memo permits a light habit-anchoring touch ("Is there a way to anchor that to something you already do — like after breakfast, or after work?") and defers full anchoring to B3 and C3. The Engineering Memo has no habit layer, no anchoring field, and no stage where it would occur — `conditions_supporting_expansion` is the nearest store but is not described as anchorable.

5. **Coaching-function count.** The Companion Memo says four of the six coaching functions are "especially central" (build supportive relationship, elicit motivation, reinforce success, support dashboard engagement). The Engineering Memo instantiates only two (`coaching_function_primary = elicit_motivation`, `coaching_function_secondary = reinforce_success`) — the relationship-building and dashboard-engagement functions have no state representation.

6. **"Score" language vs the no-score rule.** The Companion Memo's relatedness section says the Companion should be interested in "the texture of their life, not just the score" — implying a score exists — while both memos elsewhere forbid any system-computed expansion score or verdict. Read as rhetorical, but the word "score" should not survive into implementation.

## Anything unclear

- **Are the five dimensions a fixed structured checklist, or conversational territory?** The UX requirements say "Allow the Member to self-assess each dimension", implying all five are always presented; stage management says "never force all stages if the Member's reflection is shorter". Whether a member may leave a dimension entirely unassessed, and what the record holds if so, is unspecified.
- **What is `other` for?** The data model allows `dimension = other` but no memo says how a member-named dimension is created, labelled, or presented.
- **Sufficiency threshold for stage advance.** "advance only when the Member has provided sufficient response" — no definition of sufficient.
- **Prolonged disengagement threshold.** "the system detects prolonged disengagement and gracefully suspends" — no timeout, turn count, or resume behavior specified.
- **`conditions_supporting_expansion` is "free text or tagged"** — the tag vocabulary is never given.
- **`linked_module_source` attribution mechanism.** Whether the member assigns `reconnect / rewire / rebuild / c1 / mixed / unclear` or the system infers it is unstated; if inferred, it sits close to the autonomy prohibition on overriding the member's assessment.
- **How the C1 revised ReClaim List is used.** State requirements say it should be "available to connect expansion to goals", but no stage, question, or output field consumes it, and nothing writes back to the list.
- **What exactly C3 consumes.** The checklist says "Final self-assessment available to downstream ReClaim assets (C3)" without naming which fields C3 reads or whether the remaining_barriers feed a C3 planning step.
- **Barrier detection.** "When the system detects barrier-like signals" — the detection method (classifier, keyword, model judgement) is unspecified for all five signals.
- **No crisis-routing instruction appears in either memo**, despite C2 explicitly inviting grief and discouragement ("grief for what has narrowed", "recognition can be painful"). Whether C2 inherits kernel crisis routing needs confirming on our side.
- **No revisit/cadence guidance.** Nothing says whether the Bigger World Audit is retaken on the Loop, or how a second C2 relates to the first.
- **No community requirement** appears in any of the three docs.
