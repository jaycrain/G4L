### Audit Step 2 — Cross-domain priority sorting

```text
VERBATIM
Audit Step 2 - Cross-domain priority sorting

After all four domains are discussed, the goal should shift from reflection to prioritization.

Instruction to Member: "Now that we've looked at all four areas, we can try to find some synergies and priorities. The goal is not to fix everything at once but to identify what matters most right now and where progress would create the biggest shift."

Instructions to Companion: Ask the following priority questions

Q1. "Looking across these four areas, which gap feels most painful or costly to leave unaddressed?"

Q2. "Which area feels most important to your identity right now?"

Q3. "Which area are you actually most ready to work on, even if it isn't the biggest gap?"

Q4. "Which area, if it improved, would create the biggest ripple effect in the rest of your life?"

Q5. "If you could make meaningful progress in only one area over the next 30 days, which one would you choose?"
```

> **Sorting rule:** five member-answered questions, each naming ONE of the four domains. Q1 = costliest gap · Q2 = most identity-important · Q3 = highest readiness (explicitly permitted to differ from the biggest gap) · Q4 = biggest ripple · Q5 = the single 30-day choice. Note Q5 is the member's own election and is the effective tie-breaker feeding Primary Priority.

### Audit Step 3 — Priority classification

```text
VERBATIM
Audit Step 3 - Priority classification

The companion should sort responses into:

| Category | Meaning |
| Primary Priority | The area most important to address now |
| Secondary Priority | The next area that matters, but not first |
| Momentum Lever | The easiest high-value place to start |
| Key Obstacle | The main pattern blocking progress |
| First Action | A realistic next step within 7 days |
```

> **Classification rule — five output slots.** Primary Priority and Secondary Priority are **domains**. Momentum Lever is a **domain or action** ("easiest high-value place to start" — drawn from the highest-readiness answer). Key Obstacle is a **pattern**, harvested from the four Q7 Obstacle answers. First Action is a **concrete next step bounded to 7 days**, harvested from the Q8 Early action answers.

## Asset C3 — Quality Days Practice

*(No attribute table is present for C3 in the source. Its Layer is established as **Extension** by the document opening and by C4's Extension Check keyed to C3.)*

### Overview for Companion

```text
VERBATIM
Overview for Companion.

This is a 1-week self-monitoring practice in identity-based living. It is NOT a tool to track your habits but rather a tool to identify what constitutes a quality day for them AND practice in evaluating the quality of their days over a week.

The activity fits well as a companion activity to ReClaim C2 - Audit, but it has a different function. In the C2 - Audit activity, the member identifies and prioritizes gaps. This C3 - Quality Days practice teaches daily awareness, self-monitoring, and value-based choice shaping that may help them in achieving it.

The goal is NOT to necessarily change their behavior but to build competence AND confidence that they can volitionally enhance the quality of their days.

The core framing that guides this activity is as follows:

Quality Days lead to a Quality Life

A Quality Life enables and supports Quality Days

This creates a useful two-way lens:

some choices create a better day

some patterns create a better life system

over time, each reinforces the other

So the activity should not just ask, "Did you do the right things today?"

It should also ask: "What conditions helped make a quality day more likely?"

Thus, the central move is to: help a member with the following:

Ask them "What makes a day feel like a quality day for me?"

Then help the member to extend this notion and clarify the elements:

define it,

rank its ingredients,

Then provide a self-monitoring tool to help a member track it

notice it daily,

learn what supports it,

see how repeated quality days begin shaping a quality life.
```

### Conceptual structure — anchor elements vs meaning elements

```text
VERBATIM
Conceptual structure

A quality day should include two types of elements:

1. Anchor elements

These are foundational and should always be present in some form:

physical activity

healthy eating

sleep / recovery

basic self-care

These help stabilize the day.

2. Meaning elements

These are the things that make the day feel personally worthwhile:

positive social connection

peace

clarity

purpose

focus

relaxation

spiritual grounding

contribution

enjoyment

progress

creativity

These make the day feel like your day, not just a compliant day.

That distinction is important because many people can "do healthy things" without actually feeling alive, aligned, or fulfilled.

Suggested flow of the activity is in 2 steps:
```

### Step 1: Defining a Quality Day

```text
VERBATIM
Step 1: Defining a Quality Day

The Companion helps the Member answer:

When you end a genuinely good day, what made it good?

What needs to be present for a day to feel meaningful, healthy, and worthwhile?

Which elements are essential?

Which are helpful but not necessary?

The output becomes a personal Quality Day Profile.

Companion prompt example

"When you picture a day that feels genuinely good - not perfect, but solid, healthy, meaningful, and aligned - what is present in that day?"

Then:

"Let's build your version of a Quality Day. Some parts may be foundational, like movement or eating well. Others may be personal, like peace, connection, focus, or time outside. The goal is to identify what actually gives your day value."
```

#### Step 1 output: priority list (the three tiers)

```text
VERBATIM
Step 1 output: priority list

Have the Member sort items into three tiers:

| Tier | Meaning |
| Tier 1 - Essential | A day is hard to call "quality" without these |
| Tier 2 - Important | These strongly improve the day |
| Tier 3 - Bonus | These add richness, but are not required |

This is better than a flat checklist because it teaches prioritization.

Example

| Tier 1 - Essential | Tier 2 - Important | Tier 3 - Bonus |
| moved my body | had a meaningful conversation | spent time outside |
| ate reasonably well | had time to think clearly | did something creative |
| got enough rest | felt calm for part of the day | had unstructured fun |
```

> **Note the two competing structures Greg gives for the Step 1 output.** The tiered version above (Tier 1 Essential / Tier 2 Important / Tier 3 Bonus), and — later, under "Ranking method" — a simplified 3/3/2 version he explicitly recommends for app use ("This may work even better than a larger tiered list if you want it simple for app use"). The **Daily log screen table** is built on the 3/3/2 version (non-negotiables / contributors / disruptors), so the 3/3/2 model is the one that carries through to logging. **The disruptors slot exists ONLY in the 3/3/2 model — the tier model has no disruptor concept.**

### Step 2 — QD Monitoring

```text
VERBATIM
Step 2 – QD Monitoring

Each day, the Member logs 4 things:

1. Quality Day Score

"Overall, how much did today feel like a quality day?"

Scale: 1-10

2. Completed elements

Which of your Quality Day elements showed up today?

This can be a simple checklist from their personalized list.

3. Most meaningful element

"What contributed most to the quality of your day?"

This forces reflection on what mattered most.

4. Biggest missing element

"What was missing today that would have improved the day most?"

This identifies leverage for change.
```

#### Design principle — track choices, not just outcomes

```text
VERBATIM
Strong design principle: track choices, not just outcomes

Since the goal is to teach self-monitoring skills, the exercise should help people notice:

what they chose

what those choices produced

what conditions shaped those choices

So each day should connect:

choices -> lived experience -> perceived day quality

That is the actual learning loop.
```
