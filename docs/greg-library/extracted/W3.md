## Requirements

### [ID] W3-01
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** W3 is a two-component activity — the False Start protocol (relapse prevention) plus a mindfulness practice — and both must be present.
- **Verbatim:** "The first part of the activity, the False Start protocol, is well grounded in research on relapse prevention. … The second part of W3, the mindfulness practice, strengthens the person's ability to use those relapse prevention skills in real time."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** The W3 asset delivers both a protocol-building component and an in-life noticing/mindfulness component, not one alone.

### [ID] W3-02
- **Type:** posture
- **Requirement:** The activity's target is the member's *interpretation* of the lapse, not the lapse itself.
- **Verbatim:** "The real danger is often not the lapse itself, but the interpretation of the lapse. When a person treats one disruption as proof that they have failed, the setback can become self-reinforcing. W3 is designed to interrupt that process."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** Companion turns after a reported False Start address the story the member is telling about it, not adherence.

### [ID] W3-03
- **Type:** instrument
- **Requirement:** The relapse-prevention component must do four things: normalize setbacks, identify high-risk situations, recognize warning signs early, and respond in a way that restores forward motion.
- **Verbatim:** "Relapse prevention works by helping people normalize setbacks, identify high-risk situations, recognize warning signs early, and respond in a way that restores forward motion rather than deepening retreat. In other words, it teaches recovery as a skill."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** The front-end protocol flow visibly covers normalizing, high-risk identification, early-warning recognition, and a recovery move.

### [ID] W3-04
- **Type:** posture
- **Requirement:** The activity must not demand perfect adherence; it teaches staying in contact with a messy process.
- **Verbatim:** "Rather than demanding perfect adherence, W3 teaches people how to stay in contact with the process even when it becomes messy, inconsistent, or emotionally uncomfortable."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** No adherence target, completion percentage, or "perfect week" framing appears anywhere in the asset.

### [ID] W3-05
- **Type:** posture

- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** No member-facing string claims prevention; all causal claims are hedged.

## In-app summary (verbatim)

> In-app summary: W3 "Mindful Monitoring"
>
> This activity helps you stay with the process when change gets messy. Most people do not struggle because they never cared or never tried. They struggle because a false start can feel like proof that they failed, and once that story takes over it becomes much easier to stop. This exercise helps you learn a different response. The False Start part shows you how to recognize setbacks as part of real change, not the end of it. The mindfulness part helps you notice stress, discouragement, and old habits before they quietly pull you off course. Research in this area suggests that people do better when they know how to recover from lapses and when they can pay attention to themselves without immediately judging or shutting down. By doing this activity, you build the ability to notice, reset, and keep going.

## Contradictions

1. **Streaks: reinforced vs. explicitly not the reward.** The Engineering Memo requires streak tracking, dashboard streak visibility, and milestone affirmations at 3/5/7 days ("Reinforce success | Generate streak affirmations at milestones (3, 5, 7 days)"; UX #4 "Streak and pattern visibility"). The Companion Memo states "The reward here is not a streak or a score — it is the felt sense of understanding one's own patterns more clearly," and the Engineering Memo's own tracker constraint says "The tracker must not display a running score, percentage, or compliance grade." Needs a call on whether a streak counter is displayed to the member at all, or only used internally to time affirmations.

2. **Who chooses the monitoring target.** Companion Memo Step 3 prescribes the domain: "log decisions related to physical activity and eating — along with obstacles or challenges that you didn't anticipate." Both memos elsewhere insist the member chooses: "the focus of monitoring [is] the Member's to choose" and `monitoring_target = "What the Member is tracking this week"`. Is physical-activity-and-eating a fixed scope or a default the member can override?

3. **Number of triggers.** Step 1 asks four category questions and Step 2 says "For each trigger you named, write the response in advance," implying at least four. But the Companion Memo's Don't list bans "Let's make sure your protocol has at least five triggers," and Engineering says the system must not "require specific components in the Member's protocol." Whether a member may leave a category blank is unresolved.

4. **The mindfulness practice as a discrete thing.** The Science Check names "the mindfulness practice" as the second of two parts of W3. Neither memo specifies a distinct mindfulness exercise — in the memos, mindfulness is a posture applied through daily noticing and the check-in, with no separate practice, script, or duration.

## Anything unclear

- **Missed-day backup.** Stage 4 requires "Identify backup for missed days" but no doc says what a backup is (retroactive entry? next-day catch-up? a lighter one-tap entry?) or whether backfilling a prior date is permitted in the tracker.
- **Week boundary and completion.** No doc states what completes W3 — 7 elapsed days, N check-ins, or member-declared. Exit condition three ("the monitoring period has elapsed without engagement and the system gracefully suspends") implies a timer, but the suspend behavior, and whether a suspended member can resume or must restart, is unspecified.
- **"End of each cluster of days."** Summaries are required "at the end of each cluster of days" with no definition of a cluster (every 2 days? 3? on demand?).
- **`days_elapsed` vs. `missed_days`.** Both are tracked but the relationship is undefined — whether a day with no check-in increments missed_days immediately, at midnight, or only relative to the member's stated cue time.
- **Trigger `"new"` handling.** The tracker allows `trigger_fired = "new"`, and a check-in question asks "was it one you named, or something new?" — but no doc says whether a new trigger is offered for addition to the protocol (which would be a protocol_revision) or just logged.
- **Reminder channel.** UX #8 requires "optional check-in reminders anchored to the Member's chosen cue" without naming a channel (in-app, push, SMS, email).
- **Where the ReStart/protocol artifact lives.** UX #2 requires "the Member's triggers and responses visible during each check-in," but no doc says whether the protocol also persists as a durable artifact after W3 closes (e.g. into the Playbook) for use during B3.
