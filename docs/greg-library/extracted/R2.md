## Requirements

### [ID] R2-01
- **Type:** threading
- **Requirement:** R2 sits in the Excavation layer of ReConnect and is the deeper-dive/internalization step between R1 (Recognition) and R3 (application).
- **Verbatim:** "R2 sits in the Excavation layer of ReConnect and represents the deeper-dive step in the Aware → Prepare → Engage progression. Where R1 (Recognition) gives the member the awareness that a gap exists, R2 (Excavation) helps them internalize how that gap came to be — the specific life circumstances, decisions, and slow drifts that produced it. That internalization is the necessary bridge to R3, where the member turns the work outward into application and practice."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** R2 is only reachable after/alongside R1 and hands off to R3; the asset is labelled/ordered as the second ReConnect gated asset.

### [ID] R2-02
- **Type:** threading
- **Requirement:** R1 shows a gap exists; R2 asks the member to name the doors they walked through to get there.
- **Verbatim:** "R1 shows the member that a gap exists. R2 asks them to name the doors they walked through to get there."
- **Source:** Science Check
- **Testable as:** R2's opening does not re-run gap recognition; it moves straight to causal naming.

### [ID] R2-03

- **Verbatim:** "The activity presents several common doors into the Fade — the Relationship Door, the Social Door, the Autopilot Door, and others that capture recurring patterns of identity loss such as caregiving, career absorption, injury or illness, and the quiet erosion of routine."

## In-app summary (verbatim)

> This activity helps you name the specific life circumstances that drove your Fade. That matters because the Fade rarely comes from a single event — it usually comes through several doors at once, and most of them are easy to miss because each one feels reasonable in the moment. This exercise gives you a set of common doors (relationship loss, social contraction, autopilot, and others) and asks you to mark how relevant each one is to your own drift. You can mark more than one, because most people walk through several. After that, you will reflect on which door you walked through first, which has shaped you the most, whether any door is still open and being walked through right now, and what naming these doors changes about how you see your own Fade. Research in this area suggests that people adapt to life change more effectively when they can place it in a clear causal and temporal story rather than carrying it as a fog. By doing this activity, you give yourself a more honest map of how you got here — and you can share a door in the Spark space, where you will find that the Fade is a shared pattern, not a personal failure. That recognition is one of the most powerful early experiences in the program.

## Door descriptions (verbatim, Companion Memo)

- "The Social Door — your friends faded, moved away, got busy, stopped calling"

## Contradictions

1. **Closure sequence length: five steps vs four.** The Companion Memo defines the closure as a five-step sequence — "1\. Which door did you walk through first? ... 2\. Which door has had the biggest impact ... 3\. Is there a door that’s still open ... 4\. What does recognizing these doors change ... 5\. Foreshadow lightly" — and the Engineering Memo's stage structure lists Stages 4–8 (first, biggest, still open, internalization, community+foreshadow). But the Engineering Memo's implementation checklist item 5 says "Temporal reflection closure implements the four-step sequence (first, biggest, still open, internalization)", excluding the foreshadow/community step from the count.

2. **Door taxonomy is open-ended vs fixed.** The Science Check speaks of a defined presented set ("The activity presents several common doors into the Fade — the Relationship Door, the Social Door, the Autopilot Door, and others that capture recurring patterns... such as caregiving, career absorption, injury or illness, and the quiet erosion of routine"), while the Companion and Engineering memos leave the list partly system-defined and partly member-defined ("Other doors as identified by the member or added by the system"; "allow the Member to identify doors not on the standard list"). No document states a fixed door count.

3. **"Quiet erosion of routine" vs the Autopilot Door.** The Science Check lists "the quiet erosion of routine" as an additional door alongside Autopilot, but the Companion Memo defines the Autopilot Door as "decades of routine without reflection" — the two overlap and it is not stated whether they are one door or two.

## Anything unclear

- **No door count is specified.** Greg names three doors explicitly (Relationship, Social, Autopilot) and gestures at four more categories (caregiving, career absorption, injury or illness, erosion of routine) without saying how many the app should present, or whether the "others" are individually named doors or a single "other" bucket.
- **`still_open_door` field type.** The data model glosses it as "Whether any door is still open (the active Fade)" — ambiguous between a boolean and a door reference. The framing text ("That’s the active Fade. It’s the one R3’s spark will need to address") implies a door reference, and possibly more than one.
- **Whether a member can mark more than one first/biggest/still-open door.** Multi-door marking is explicit for the ratings; the temporal roles are phrased in the singular but the cumulative logic ("most people walk through several") does not obviously stop at one.
- **What "the Fade Doors framing has been presented" gates.** It is listed as a trigger condition for engagement while also being the first thing the opening stage delivers — unclear whether the framing is a pre-activity screen or the Companion's first turn.
- **Where the pre-reflection summary lives.** It is required both as a Companion-generated conversational summary and as a "Pre-reflection summary view" UI surface; whether these are one thing or two is not stated.
- **Relationship of R2's Spark-space share to the community record.** `community_share_accepted` is captured, but no document specifies what the shared artifact contains (the door name only, the member's language, or both) or whether it is attributed.
