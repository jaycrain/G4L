## QUESTION 1 · Does "somewhat relevant" earn a full excavation?

**What the board does now.** Each Door is rated on your three-point scale (R2-04: 1 = not relevant, 2 = somewhat
relevant, 3 = very relevant). Anything rated **2 or 3** is treated as claimed, and every claimed Door is then
walked in full.

**What that produced.** Jennifer rated all eleven cards and marked ten:

| | count |
| --- | --- |
| 3 · very relevant | 7 |
| 2 · somewhat relevant | 3 |
| never touched | 0 |

Greg marked nine the same way (four 3s, five 2s). Donna marked four.

Jennifer's Session reached 132 turns across two sittings and she did not finish. Afterwards she wrote:

> *"I honestly didn't think I selected all those doors. Could have been my error though."*

**Our reading of why.** She was not selecting — she was rating. The board asks "How much is this yours?" and she
answered it honestly, eleven times. The app then converted every answer of 2 or above into a commitment to walk
that Door in full. She was never told that marking committed her to anything.

**The question for you.** R2-05 explicitly invites marking more than one, and R2-06 says there is no hierarchy
among Doors. Both argue against us silently dropping her 2s. But a 2 and a 3 clearly meant different things to
her, and the instrument records that difference and then discards it.

Some options, in the order we would rank them — but this is your call:

1. **Say the commitment out loud on the board.** Keep every marked Door, and make it visible: "you've marked ten
   — we'll take each one." Ten becomes her informed choice rather than a surprise. Preserves R2-05/06 exactly.
2. **Only 3s are walked; 2s are recorded and available.** A one-line change. Jennifer would have walked seven.
   Our hesitation: it silently discards Doors she said were partly hers, and that is close to deciding for her.
3. **Ask her, after the board.** "You've marked ten. Do you want to take all of them, or start with the ones that
   weigh most?" Costs a turn; makes depth her choice rather than a threshold's.

**Note that seven is the harder number.** Even if 2s stopped counting, Jennifer has seven Doors at "very
relevant" — a parent's decline, a diagnosis, a career cliff, an empty nest. Her board is honest. Whatever we do
about the 2s, a member can legitimately arrive with seven, and we would like your view on whether seven Doors of
excavation is one session in the sense R2-17 means ("a single session, not a sustained monitoring period").

## QUESTION 2 · Identity claimed at Excavation, rather than at intake

**Background.** At intake a member is asked to name who they are reclaiming — the handle ("the Athlete", "the
Maker"). Some cannot, and we let them defer: `identitySkipped` means "name it later, at Excavation." That is the
right escape — being unable to name it is common and is not a failure — but **the later half was never built.**
A member who defers currently has no route back to it. Jay's own account has a null handle for this reason.

**Why Excavation is where it belongs, and why we want your view.** By the end of R2 a member has walked their
Doors and heard the cumulative pattern read back. That is the first moment in the program where the material for
a name actually exists — she has just described who she was before the Doors opened, in her own words, several
times over. Asking at intake asks her to name someone she has not yet been reminded of.

**What we would want to know:**

- **Is Excavation the right beat**, or does the naming belong to R3 (where the Fade's cost is stated) or later?
- **Where in R2** — after the cumulative pattern (R2-34), or after the internalization question (R2-07/R2-38)?
- **What is the ask?** We would not want to hand her a list to pick from; the identity is hers to coin. But
  "what would you call her?" cold is the question she already could not answer at intake.
- **What happens if she still cannot?** Our instinct is that "not yet" must remain a permanent, dignified answer
  and never be asked a third time — but the program routes on the Reclaim List, not the handle, so nothing is
  blocked by its absence. Confirm that is how you see it.

## QUESTION 3 · B1 asks for a rating before there is anything to rate with

**What happens.** In *What's Your Why*, a member is shown your framing and the first item together —

> *"There's a simple place to start — your why. The reasons that are actually yours. Answer a few questions to get
> a read on where you stand today. For each statement, tell me how true it feels — 1 (not at all true for you) to
> 7 (very true for you)."*

— and then a turn or two later is shown **the same framing and the same item again**, that time as the actual
assessment with the 1–7 control. Donna, walking it: *"This is odd sequencing. It asks the question and the first
time it asks me to rate but with no chips. Then it asks it again as part of the assessment."*

**The cause is exact**, and it is ours: that framing-plus-first-item block is used as the hand-in of **two
consecutive stages** — the doorway into the talk beat, and the talk beat into the instrument. It fires at both.

**Why we have not just fixed it.** Removing one of the two changes the ORDER of your instrument — which of the
doorway, the elicitation, and the first item comes when. We have got that wrong before: C2 once ran your Q1–Q8
out of sequence and it read to a member as a different assessment. R2-29 is explicit that the door and its
description precede the rating question, and we assume B1 has an equivalent intent we should not guess at.

**The question:** what is the intended order at B1's opening — the frame, the evocation ("what makes you want to
move?"), and the first rated item? Specifically, should the 1–7 framing be spoken **once, immediately before the
first item**, with the talk beat carrying no rating language at all? That is our reading, and it is a small change
if you confirm it.
