## 1 · THE HEADLINE — the Legacy Letter is live, and it is the member's own writing

**What a member does.** At the end of Reconnect, after the Window beat, they are asked to write a letter **to
themselves, dated one year from today**. The Companion asks a few of Greg's six prompts, drafts the letter **in
their words**, and they revise it until it is theirs. It saves to the Playbook under *Who you are*. It is **not**
locked until its date — the date is a dedication, not a timer, and they can change the letter whenever it stops
being true.

This closes a gap Greg named walking the product himself: *"I thought it would also task me with actually writing
a Legacy Letter… I never really wrote it."* It also changes the shape of the first phase — a member now leaves
Reconnect holding a **destination**, not only a diagnosis.

**THE QUOTE/DESCRIBE SPLIT IS UNUSUALLY IMPORTANT HERE.** The six prompts are **authored** (Greg's, in his order)
and quote verbatim. **The letter itself is model-drafted from the member's own answers — it is DYNAMIC.** Never
quote one as canonical, even though it will read like the most quotable artifact in the product. Describe it
instead: first person, half a page, from them to the version of them who kept going, their own words kept even
where plain or awkward, ending on the unfinished business if they named one. It never praises, promises an
outcome, or adds anything they did not say.

**New authored copy around it** (quotable; in the transcript):
- *"One last thing."* / *"I'd like you to write a letter — to yourself, a year from today. Not a plan and not a
  pep talk. The letter you'd want to be handed in a year by the version of you who kept going."*
- *"Read it back. What's not right — a line that isn't how you'd say it, or something missing?"*
- *"Saved — dated a year from today, and addressed to you."*

## 2 · THE SECOND HEADLINE — the Companion points members at each other

**What changed.** When a Session ends, above the Continue button, a member may see **one line naming a real
person**. Not an invitation to a place — a fact about someone already in relationship with them:

- a pact gone quiet: *"Sarah is holding you to ride the loop again. It's been 5 days since you two checked in."*
- an unread reply: *"Mark replied to what you wrote about the mornings."*
- and for someone who has never been in, the smallest possible ask: *"There are people here doing this at the same
  time as you. You can read without saying anything."*

**When there is nothing true to say, it says nothing.** Most members, most Sessions, see no line at all.

**Why it matters for how we describe the product** — Jay: *"We want to emphasize the human side that exists on the
app. And it's a credibility builder for the Companion to encourage human interaction. Loss of connection is a huge
factor in midlife loneliness and identity loss."* This is the Companion's stated north star — bridging members
toward real people, never replacing them — visible for the first time.

**It never sells the Community.** If marketing describes this, describe it as **naming a person, not promoting a
place**. A pitch from the Companion would spend the credibility the feature exists to build.

## 5 · FUNCTION — what the product does that it did not

**Sessions remember what came before them.** Six Sessions (W1, W2, W3, B3, C1, C3) now open holding the member's
earlier work — their why, their skills map, the true lines they wrote, the picture they built, their Doors, their
Legacy Letter — and refer to **one** of them where it genuinely bears. Built from Greg's own
`prior_module_context` declarations across all twelve engineering memos. Describe it as **the program
remembering**, never as tracking.

**The Doors are a profile, not a list.** A member can say how much a Door weighs, which opened first, and
crucially **which is still open** — the active Fade. Greg: *"a door that is still open is the active Fade."*

**B3's monitoring week is real.** The member tells the Companion how a day went and it is written down — Greg's
seven fields, including what made it easy or hard and whether eating and moving affected each other. The Companion
reads it back, so a Tuesday described is a Tuesday remembered.

**B2's tracker is personal** — the three skills their own assessment marked as having the most room, instead of a
generic *"Noticed a skill"* row.

**The Bigger World Audit** takes taps instead of typing for the four areas, and its close now says what happens
with the area they chose: *"That's your first focus now. I'll work from it, and it's what we'll build your Quality
Days around."*

## Verifying this drop

`MANIFEST.md` carries a sha256 and byte count per part. The parts are in git, so there is no partial state that
looks complete — the failure that silently lost the transcript twice in August is structurally gone.

**Quotability, unchanged:** quote the authored (transcript, assessment items, UI, badges) verbatim; describe the
Companion's in-the-moment reflections by the voice rules rather than quoting them — they vary per member.

**One extraction artifact, so you don't "fix" it:** a few lines in the transcript show `\u2019` where a curly
apostrophe belongs (e.g. *"or tell me and I\u2019ll mark it"*). That is a JavaScript escape in our source that
renders correctly to the member — the extractor just does not decode it. The member sees `I'll`. Do not treat it
as a typo, and do not quote the escape.

**The caveat from v3.4.6 still stands:** the transcript can include per-turn model steering, which no member ever
sees — anything beginning *"RIGHT NOW:"* or *"CURRENT STAGE:"* is an instruction to the model, not copy. Do not
reconcile or quote those.

## 8 · THE TRANSCRIPT ITSELF WAS INCOMPLETE — please re-read it, not just the diff

This bundle's transcript is **regenerated with a fixed extractor**, and it is the more important half of this drop.

The extractor only ever read **quoted string literals**. Any member copy written as JSX text — the words between
tags, which is how every page headline and paragraph in this app is authored — was invisible to it unless the
opening and closing tag happened to sit on the same line. So multi-line prose had never reached canon at all.

**What was missing, and had always been missing:**
- **The public landing page** (`app/page.tsx`) — the first words anyone reads, including everyone arriving from a
  marketing link. It had never been in a bundle.
- **The onboarding hero** — "You didn't lose yourself, you stopped looking."
- **Jay's own Clip in definition**, which the previous note told you to quote verbatim. It was in the rejected
  pile, not the transcript.
- The identity picker, the email-verification page, and the Companion hero.

Also fixed: glossary rows are authored as a bolded term plus a lowercase continuation, so each row read as a
sentence fragment and was thrown out. They are now joined the way a member reads them — *"Clip in — our founder's
cycling metaphor…"*, *"Reconnect — start looking at who you are."*

**What this means for you:** if you reconciled the glossary from any earlier bundle, you were working from a
transcript missing its front door and its own vocabulary rows. Worth a fresh pass rather than a diff.
